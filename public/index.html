The problem

We have data and learners. We still need knowledge we can trust.

EVOKE starts from a practical gap: we do not yet have a reliable way to elicit the structured and reusable claims a person holds, then prove that what we extracted is correct and useful.

The workshop focuses on elicitation into representations with formal, provable, or sound inference properties: constraints, optimization models, logic, programs, typed specifications, ontologies, and probabilistic models.

Its central question separates knowledge from data: how do we know the elicited knowledge is correct and useful?

Why formal targets matter

When knowledge lands in a formal representation, evaluation becomes measurable.

01

A solver can return ground truth for the elicited structure.

02

A verifier can certify whether a result follows from the representation.

03

An unsatisfiable core can expose a contradiction the expert did not know they held.

The frontier

Language models made elicitation cheap. Soundness is still the hard part.

Large language models have collapsed the cost of the natural-language interface that stalled knowledge acquisition for decades. But prose is not a proof, and a plausible specification that is subtly wrong is the dangerous case.

Compile to form

Measure utility

Intellectual frame

Educability, explicit knowledge, and belief verification.

EVOKE draws on Leslie Valiant's educability framework, which locates human uniqueness not in learning from examples but in being taught explicit knowledge and chaining it, with belief verification as the safeguard.

The workshop convenes researchers and practitioners around shared evaluation, a solver-verifiable shared task, and the question of when extracted knowledge is provably useful.
